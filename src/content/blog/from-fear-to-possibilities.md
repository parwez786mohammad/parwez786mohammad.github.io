During the first few weeks of my first semester at engineering college, the same ritual played out every day. A few students would walk to the front of the class, face everyone and introduce themselves. Name, hometown, a smile, and back to their seat. For them, it took thirty seconds.

For me, it was impossible. Day after day, I stayed in my seat, too afraid to speak in front of a room full of strangers. By the time the introductions were over, I was the only student in the class who had never introduced himself.

**More than a decade later, on 15 September 2026, I walked to the front of a room in Dubai, faced a full audience at DSO Toastmasters Club, and finally did it.**

This post is the story of that journey: why I believe Toastmasters is one of the best platforms for personal growth, why I joined it as a manager, the path I have committed to, and how I chose to deliver my very first speech, the Ice Breaker. You can watch the speech itself further down.

## Why Toastmasters is a great platform

Most of us never really learn how to speak in public. We are simply thrown into it: a class presentation, a job interview, a client meeting or a team briefing. Some of us manage, many of us struggle, and very few of us ever get a safe place to practice before it really matters.

Toastmasters fills exactly that gap. Since 1924, it has helped people around the world become more confident speakers and better leaders, not through lectures or theory, but through regular practice in a supportive club.

What makes it work is a simple, powerful cycle:

* **Practice.** Every meeting gives you a chance to speak, whether it is a prepared speech, an unexpected Table Topics question or a meeting role such as timer, evaluator or host.
* **Feedback.** Every speech is evaluated by a fellow member, so you leave each meeting knowing what you did well and what to improve.
* **Structure.** The Pathways program turns all of this into a clear learning path of five levels, with each project building one specific skill.
* **Support.** Everyone in the room is there to grow, so mistakes are not judged. They are simply the next lesson.

Practice, feedback, structure and support. That combination is exactly what I was missing, and it is what brought me through the door.

## Why I joined as a manager

As a Project Manager, communication sits at the center of almost everything I do. In a single day, I may brief my team on priorities, update a client on progress, chair a review meeting and give feedback on someone's work. Over the years, I have learned that a project rarely succeeds on a good plan alone. It succeeds when that plan is communicated clearly to the people who deliver it.

That realization is what led me to DSO Toastmasters Club in Dubai. I joined with three clear goals.

**1. Convey my message clearly.** A team performs best when everyone understands not just what we are doing, but why. I want every message I deliver, to my team, my management or a client, to be simple, structured and easy to act on.

**2. Learn to give effective feedback.** Feedback is one of the most powerful tools a manager has. Delivered well, it builds confidence and helps people grow. Delivered poorly, it does the opposite. Since every speech in Toastmasters is evaluated, there is no better place to learn how to give feedback that is honest, specific and encouraging.

**3. Become a good mentor.** This goal is the most personal one. I know how much mentorship really helps, because it has helped shape the person I am today. The guidance and encouragement I received at different stages of my life opened doors I could never have opened alone. Now I want to do the same for others, and I want to learn this skill in the best possible way.

With these goals clear, the next step was choosing the right path.

## My path: Dynamic Leadership and a two-year commitment

In Toastmasters, every member chooses a Pathways learning path that matches their goals. I chose **Dynamic Leadership**.

The choice felt natural. My goals are not only about speaking well on a stage; they are about leading people well at work. Dynamic Leadership focuses on exactly that: communicating with purpose, guiding people through change and growing as a leader. It connects directly to the three goals that brought me to Toastmasters.

I have also made a commitment to myself. For the next two years, I will dedicate time to learning and to being actively involved with my club: preparing and delivering speeches, taking on meeting roles, evaluating others and supporting fellow members on their own journeys. **Growth comes from consistency, not from one good speech.**

## Project 1: The Ice Breaker

The Ice Breaker is the very first speech every Toastmaster gives. It lasts 4 to 6 minutes, and its purpose is simple: to introduce yourself to the club and learn the basic structure of a public speech.

Behind that simple purpose, the project lays some important foundations:

* **Speech structure.** Organizing your thoughts into a clear opening, body and conclusion.
* **Preparation with spontaneity.** Preparing well while still sounding natural, rather than reciting a memorized script.
* **Standing before an audience.** For many members, this is the first time they speak alone in front of a group.
* **Receiving feedback.** Every Ice Breaker is evaluated. You learn to accept feedback graciously and choose one point to apply in your next speech.

Members are free to talk about anything: their background, their work, their hobbies or why they joined. For me, though, this speech carried extra weight. Introducing myself was the very thing I had once been too afraid to do.

### How the speech was structured

In short, the speech tells the story of a village boy who was too afraid to introduce himself, froze on stage in college, and slowly turned that fear into a career, before revealing that the boy was me. It was built in seven parts:

1. **The hook.** It opens with "Imagine a young boy…" and introduces him only in the third person: a humble village family where no one had been to school, and a father determined to educate his son.
2. **The fear.** At engineering college, he is the only student who never introduces himself, because he is afraid of speaking to strangers.
3. **The low point.** Forced to speak in the college auditorium, he freezes, forgets every line, says "Sorry" and walks off the stage.
4. **The turning point.** That night, he promises himself that fear will never define his future, and months later he becomes the topper of the entire college.
5. **The journey.** A job with a leading US-based company, a move to a new country and, finally, the role of Project Manager.
6. **The reveal.** "Ladies and gentlemen, yes, that boy is me. And today, finally, I would like to introduce myself."
7. **The introduction and close.** Only then do I share my name, my roots, my life outside work and my ambition, ending with the line that sums up the whole speech: *it is not where you start, but how far you are willing to go.*

## Why I did it differently

So that's my Ice Breaker. I didn't just deliver it well; it also won me the **Best Speaker award** of the evening. 🏆

A bit of context: while preparing, I watched quite a few Ice Breaker speeches online, and I noticed that most of them follow the same pattern. Walk up, say "Hi, I'm so-and-so", and list your job and hobbies. There is nothing wrong with that; it is the traditional way. But I wanted to try something different.

I have always believed that people connect with a story far more than with a list of facts. They don't just hear a story; they feel it, and they remember it much longer. So instead of opening with "Hi, I'm Parwez", I told my own story in the third person: a boy who froze on stage during a college speech, went completely blank and walked off in silence.

I did that on purpose. I wanted the audience to feel that fear with the boy before they knew it was me. Because that is what Toastmasters is about: not hiding the moments we froze, but owning them, and showing what came after.

That boy is now a Project Manager, leading teams and delivering high-stakes projects. Not because the fear disappeared, but because I stopped letting it decide what I do. The boy who could not introduce himself in a classroom finally did it, on a Toastmasters stage, by choice.

## Proud of where I come from

Telling this story on stage was not easy. It meant sharing my fears and failures with a room full of people I barely knew. But that is exactly why it mattered.

From a village in Bihar, to an engineering college in Bhopal, to leading projects in Dubai, my path has not been easy. Every step came with its own fears and setbacks. But I am truly proud of it: proud of my roots, proud of my father's vision for his son, and proud of every small step that brought me here.

My journey is far from over. I believe the best is still ahead, and I hope my story can serve as an example: if you work hard and refuse to let fear stop you, you can achieve big things, no matter where you start.

It is the same message I closed my Ice Breaker with:

> **It is not where you start, but how far you are willing to go.**
